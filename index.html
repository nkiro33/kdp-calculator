How KDP royalties work in 2026

Paperback

On Amazon.com you earn 60% of the list price if the book is listed at $9.99 or more, and 50% if it's listed below $9.99. Amazon then subtracts the printing cost:

royalty = list price × rate − printing cost

Printing cost depends on page count, ink and trim size. Large trim means wider than 6.12 in (155.5 mm) or taller than 9 in (228.6 mm).

Example: a 120-page black-ink 6 × 9 paperback costs $1.00 + 120 × $0.012 = $2.44 to print. At $9.99 it earns $9.99 × 60% − $2.44 = $3.55 per sale. At $8.99 it earns only $8.99 × 50% − $2.44 = $2.06, so that dollar off the price costs you $1.49 a copy.

Hardcover

Hardcovers earn 60% of the list price minus printing. Black-ink hardcovers cost $6.80 to print up to 108 pages (large trim $7.49), and $5.65 plus $0.012 per page from 110 to 550 pages (large trim $0.017 per page). A 300-page regular-trim hardcover costs $9.25 to print, so at $24.99 it earns $5.74.

Kindle eBook

Kindle books choose between two royalty options:

  • 70% for list prices from $2.99 to $12.99 on Amazon.com, after a delivery cost of $0.15 per MB. The top of this band was $9.99 until July 7, 2026, so older calculators still get $10–$12.99 books wrong.
  • 35% of the list price for anything outside that range, with no delivery cost.

70% royalty = (list price − $0.15 × MB) × 0.70

A 1 MB eBook at $12.99 earns $8.99 per sale, but the same book at $13.00 earns only $4.55. Mind the cliff. To qualify for 70%, the eBook also has to be priced at least 20% below the list price of your print edition. In Brazil, Japan, Mexico and India it also has to be enrolled in KDP Select.

Questions

Does this include Expanded Distribution?

No. The calculator uses Amazon marketplace rates. Expanded Distribution to bookstores and libraries pays a lower rate.

Why is my KDP dashboard royalty slightly different?

Amazon rounds printing costs and charges eBook delivery on the exact file size. For other marketplaces it uses local prices, VAT and printing costs. Use this to compare prices and formats, then confirm the final figure on KDP's pricing page.

Should I price my paperback at $9.99?

Often, yes. At $9.99 the rate jumps from 50% to 60%, so going from $9.49 to $9.99 can add more than a dollar per copy. Check what competing books in your niche cost before you decide.

Do Kindle Unlimited page reads count?

Not in this calculator. KU pays per page read from a monthly fund, at roughly $0.004 to $0.005 per page, and that's separate from sales royalties.
